Do We Need More Houses, or Houses We Can Afford?

Posted Oct. 27, 2026 · Updated Oct. 27, 2026 · Page look updated Nov. 2, 2026
What they say

"Build more near trains and buses, and homes get cheaper."

Sen. John Curtis is promoting a federal bill, the Build HUBS Act, that gives cheaper government loans to builders near transit stops.

What the bill (S. 3636) does

Cheaper loans for builders near stations

1. The government has a big pot of money it lends for roads and train tracks.

2. This bill lets builders borrow from it to build homes and shops within a 10-minute walk of a train or bus station.

3. If more than half the homes are priced for families who earn less than most neighbors, the builder pays only half the normal interest. Borrow $100, pay about $2.25 extra a year instead of $4.50.

4. The bill doesn't say how long those homes have to stay cheaper.

5. Builders with no cheaper homes can still borrow, just not at half price.

6. Some environmental checking gets skipped for turning offices into homes or building on land already used for roads and trains.

7. Your town still decides what can be built where.

What happened in Utah

Utah already built a lot. Prices kept climbing.

Utah built more homes in 2020–2022 than ever before.

Since 2016 a typical house went up

+124%

A typical paycheck went up

+52%
Who can buy

9 out of 10 renters can't buy a typical home.

To buy the typical Utah home, a family needs about $146,800 a year. The typical family makes about $96,658. That's about $50,000 short.

What "affordable" turned out to mean

Utah's own transit projects

Kimball Junction: 885 homes. 385 are full market price. Some "affordable" homes are for people earning up to about $128,520 a year.

The Point: "affordable" apartments end up around $1,600–$1,900 a month after district taxes and fees.

Who pays

Schools, neighbors and buyers

In these zones, most of the new property tax from a project can stay with the project for 25 years instead of going to schools and the county. Buyers in special districts pay an extra yearly charge on their home. The new bill adds federal loans at half the normal interest rate.

Is it law?

Not yet

The bill is sitting in a Senate committee. Supporters want it added to the big federal transportation bill, which is how it could pass without its own vote.

We've seen this before in Utah

Housing bills that raised what the public pays

Since 2019 Utah lawmakers passed bills to build more, sold as helping housing: special taxing districts on new homes (PIDs), and zones near transit that keep new property taxes for the project instead of schools.

Debt for those districts is now about $5.3 billion, paid by homeowners and renters inside them. Bills that would have shown the public more, like making district board members disclose conflicts, did not pass.

How much tax money these zones take

About $2.49 billion, just in the projects we've documented

When a zone is created, most of the new property tax from that area goes back to the project instead of schools, the county, fire and water. Some of the biggest:

Vineyard: about $900 million
Eagle Mountain data center zone: about $779 million
West Weber inland port area: about $343 million
Golden Spike inland port area: about $131 million
Stratos data center area next door (MIDA): total never disclosed
Lehi train station zone: $122 million
Nordic Village (Ogden Valley): up to $55 million

Statewide, the Utah State Tax Commission says $304.7 million of property taxes went to increment in 2024 alone, 6% of all property taxes, up 83% since 2015.

That doesn't count the $5.3 billion in special-district debt homeowners are paying on top.

Every state but Arizona allows this kind of tax increment financing, and the Build HUBS bill would apply nationwide.

Help, harm or neutral?

A bit of each

Could help: more homes near stations, on land already used, with loans that get paid back.

Could harm: cheaper loans with no promise the homes stay cheaper, less environmental review, and no required public report on who gets the loans.

Neutral: your town still controls zoning.

Also in the record

Rents and the bill's limits

New apartments have helped rents: apartment rents dropped 2.3% in two years. The bill keeps zoning local and asks that most of the cheaper-loan homes be affordable. But it doesn't say for how long.
