Who's moving in

Weber grows mostly from births, not newcomers

Census population estimates put Weber among Utah's lowest net-migration counties. Net migration was negative in 2022–2023, and in 2023–2024 it made up about 9% of growth. The rest was births. A "too many newcomers" explanation for Weber prices doesn't match those numbers.

Prices vs. paychecks

Home prices outran incomes

Weber County median household income $72,206 (2019) to $90,005 (2024), Census ACS. Ogden Valley median single-family sale $577,000 (2019) to $1,140,000 (2024), Mountain Luxury market reports. County income is used because no Ogden Valley income figure is published.

Ogden Valley

Ogden Valley City's draft General Plan (March 2026) puts the median home value at $839,800 (2024 ACS) against a county area income of $88,515. Moderate-income households can afford homes up to about $300,000; only 709 homes, 17% of the valley's housing, are at or below that.

Ogden city

December 2025: median sale price $495,000, median rent $1,655. Buying took an income of $125,216; renting took $66,196, an 89% gap. Nationally the gap was 46% (Redfin, via Stacker).

"Affordable" apartments

What income-restricted means in Ogden

  • Lotus Company operates about 500 income-restricted units in Ogden, with about 300 more in the pipeline, targeting 60% of area median income (KUER, Jan. 2, 2026).
  • The rent: a one-bedroom at 60% of area income rents for $1,356 a month. A single renter at the top of the eligible income range ($73,860, based on HUD's 2025 Ogden-Clearfield median of $123,100) pays 32% of gross income, above the 30% guide.
  • Flat rent: in these tax-credit buildings, rent is set at the income limit, not each tenant's income, so renters who earn less pay a larger share.
  • New bond allocations (July 8, 2026): Railyard Lofts, Ogden, $17,370,650 for 168 units (Pedcor Development Associates); Golden Links Manor, Ogden, $2,639,000 for 31 units (Related Affordable).
  • A different model: Golden Links Manor is existing Section 8 senior and disabled housing where rent is based on each tenant's income, so it works differently from new tax-credit construction.

What it means for Ogden homeowners

How would the Build HUBS bill help homeowners in Ogden?

For people who already own homes in Ogden, the bill text has nothing aimed at them. It gives no property tax relief, does nothing about PID assessments or disclosure, and offers no help buying a home. Every benefit in it goes to the borrower building the project.

What could reach Ogden

  • Union Station Neighborhood fits the bill closely. It sits right next to Ogden's FrontRunner station, with UTA and J. Fisher Companies on the team. If it borrowed under Build HUBS, the builder would get a federal loan, at half the Treasury rate if most units are at or below 80% of area income. That's about $100,900 for a family of four in the Salt Lake metro.
  • Those units would mostly be rentals, priced in the same 60–80% band as Lotus's buildings. That's not the 0–30% level where Weber's own data showed the shortage, and the bill sets no time limit on keeping them affordable.
  • New apartments can ease rents a bit. Statewide, apartment rents fell 2.3% over two years as new supply opened. Ogden renters might feel that; homeowners wouldn't see it on their bills.

What could cost Ogden homeowners

  • Stacking with local tools: if a project pairs the federal loan with a CRA or HTRZ, new property tax growth around the station goes back to the project for decades. Schools, the county and other districts then cover growth costs from everyone else's rates. That's the same pattern in Weber's $146 million paid as increment since 2015.
  • Less review: projects near the station that convert offices or build on rail and road land would skip federal environmental review, so neighbors get less notice.
  • The loan discount: the half-price interest is money the federal government doesn't collect, and every taxpayer, Ogden homeowners included, covers that.
Bottom line from the record

The bill helps builders borrow cheaply near stations. Ogden homeowners get no direct benefit. What they pay depends on whether the city stacks a CRA or HTRZ on top, and the Union Station plans haven't disclosed one yet.
