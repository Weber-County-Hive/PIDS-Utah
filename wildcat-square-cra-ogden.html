Wildcat Square: $3.3 Million in Future Taxes for Student Housing at Weber State

Ogden's Redevelopment Agency wants up to $3.3 million in future taxes over 15 years to help build student housing on a Weber State practice field. Most of it would come from Ogden School District. The public hearing is Nov. 17.

Where
Weber State University, Ogden. About 6.6 acres on the southwest part of the main campus, between West Campus Drive, Village Drive, Playfield Way and the OGX bus line. Now used as practice fields.
Who
Ogden City Redevelopment Agency (the City Council sits as its board), Weber State University, Keller Development Group (builder), and the three tax bodies asked to give up part of their share: Ogden School District, Ogden City and Weber County.
Short version
  • The plan: Weber State leases part of a practice field to Keller Development Group for 99 years. Keller builds student apartments (Ogden RDA plan and budget, Oct. 13, 2026).
  • The ask: up to $3,330,116 in future taxes from the new building, over 15 years, goes to Ogden's Redevelopment Agency (RDA) instead of the usual tax offices (hearing notice).
  • Who gives up the money: Ogden School District (85% of its share, $2,194,353), Ogden City (95%, $775,203) and Weber County (50%, $360,560). The school district's share is about 2 of every 3 dollars.
  • The city's reason: the land is state land and pays no tax today. The builder is paid back only after the building is up, and there is a hard cap.
  • Residents' concerns in May: losing campus open space, the public cost, and drawing growth away from downtown (City Weekly).
  • The "but-for" question: state law asks whether the project would happen without the tax money. The plan says no, but doesn't show the math. The city's own slide puts the whole project at $40 million; the builder's share of the tax money is about 7% of that (Oct. 13 slides).
  • Papers that don't match: 103 units or 513 beds; a 99- or 100-year lease; April 21 or May 12 for the first vote; $4.6 million or $4.87 million in site costs; and more.
  • The builder: Keller Development Group, a Utah company led by Casey Keller that already owns student housing near campus. A company named "Wildcat Square, LLC," with the same manager, was formed in December 2024 (Utah business records).
  • Hive grade: C− (Transparency C−, Financial accountability C−, Environmental C).
  • Hearing: Tuesday, Nov. 17, 2026, 6 p.m., Ogden City Council Chambers, 3rd floor, 2549 Washington Blvd.

How it works

A tax on a building that sits on state land

Weber State's land is state land, so it pays no property tax. But when a private company uses state land to make money, Utah charges a privilege tax. It is figured like a property tax.

Utah's law for these project areas counts a privilege tax as a "property tax" (Utah Code 17C-1-102(54)(b); read on a copy of the code, not yet on le.utah.gov). So the new building's privilege tax can be set aside the same way property tax is in other project areas.

The plan calls the area a Community Reinvestment Area (CRA). For 15 years, most of the new tax goes to the RDA. After that, all of it goes back to the normal tax offices for the rest of the lease.

The plan says the shares are set by state law. If the building pays more tax than expected, the extra goes back to the tax offices unless the RDA board votes to change the budget.

Why the plan says the money is needed

An old fill site, up to 38 feet deep

The plan says the site was used to dump soil and debris from other campus building projects. It says the fill is up to 38 feet deep, more than 21,000 cubic yards of bad soil would have to be hauled off, and the building area must be dug down to native soil about 18 feet below the surface.

It also points to high building costs and lower rents in Weber County than at the University of Utah, BYU or Utah State. The plan says without the tax money, the project "might not reasonably be expected to occur in the foreseeable future."

The "but-for" test: would it happen anyway?

Utah's law for these plans requires "the rationale for the use of tax increment," including an analysis of whether the project "might reasonably be expected to occur in the foreseeable future without the use of tax increment" (Utah Code 17C-5-105(13); FindLaw copy current as of Jan. 1, 2025). This is often called the "but-for" test.

What the plan gives

A statement that the project "might not reasonably be expected to occur" without the tax money, the site-cost list, rising building costs (tariffs, metals, concrete), and lower rents in Weber County than at the U of U, BYU or Utah State.

What it doesn't give

The side-by-side math: total cost, rents, loan and the builder's expected return, with and without the tax money. The city's Oct. 13 slide says the project is a $40 million total investment, so the $2,830,598 for the builder is about 7% of it (slides).

Rent

Wildcat Square's own website says rent will start "in the upper $500's" and the building will open in summer 2028 (wildcatsquare.com). It doesn't say per bed or per unit, or per month.

What Weber State told the state in 2024

In 2024, the project was described as needing no public money

Before any tax money from Ogden was discussed, Weber State asked the Utah Board of Higher Education to approve its partnership with Keller Development Group. These are from Weber State's Oct. 28, 2024 request letter (signed by Norm Tarbox, Senior Vice President for Administrative Services), the Commissioner's Nov. 21, 2024 memo and a Nov. 1, 2024 letter from the Attorney General's Office (Board of Higher Education, Nov. 21, 2024 meeting):

  • Weber State "released an RFP in spring 2023," and "The Keller Development Group was awarded the project."
  • About 500 beds; "Cost to Developer: ~$46,803,000"; estimated completion summer 2027.
  • "Cost to University: ~$3,000,000 to relocate playfields," plus parking and amenities paid from "a gift of $1.7 million" from Goldenwest Credit Union.
  • "No funding will be provided to KDG by WSU." The letter says the project "will be a minimal cost to WSU and is financially feasible."
  • Weber State gets "an initial annual rent amount of $70,000" a year for the land, with an inflation increase, and is "not subject to any guarantees."
  • The lease is "for a 99 year term, with the opportunity for five additional ten year terms." Weber State said the deal lets it "transfer development and ownership risk to a third party."
  • This is called "the first phase"; "Potentially, up to 3,000 new beds could be built as part of this long-term partnership."

The question this raises
In 2024 the project was presented to the state as financially feasible with a cost to the developer of about $46.8 million and no mention of tax money from Ogden. In 2026, the same project is presented to Ogden as unlikely to happen without $2.83 million in tax money, with a $40 million total investment on the city's slide. The plan points to higher building costs since then. What changed, and by how much, is a fair question for the hearing.

We have not yet found the Board of Higher Education's minutes showing its vote. The Commissioner recommended approval.

Both sides, from the record

What supporters and residents said

The case for it
  • The land pays nothing today. The city estimates Ogden would get more than $66,000 a year after the 15 years, for the remaining 84 years of the lease, and about $25 million over that time at 3% growth (city transmittal).
  • Weber State has 1,001 beds on campus for more than 33,000 students, with full housing and waitlists over 100, per the plan.
  • Weber State says it can add housing "without requiring university bonding or public construction funding."
  • Vice Chair Dave Graf: "This is an opportunity to turn a piece of ground that doesn't generate any tax revenue for the city at all into a piece of ground that does…" (City Weekly)
  • WSU Vice President Mark Halverson: "Our beds are full, we have long wait lists, so we really need to attract those students." (City Weekly)
Concerns raised
  • Resident Travis Pate: "I think this is another cart before the horse… Let them do some site drillings." (City Weekly)
  • Pate also asked the city to "invest in downtown" and said the project could pull growth away from it.
  • Other residents raised the loss of open green space on campus and the public cost (Citizen Portal).
  • Most of the money is the school district's share, and the district's board votes on it separately from the city.
Papers that don't match

Seven places the city's own documents differ

These may have simple answers. They are fair questions for the hearing.

1 · Units or beds

The plan calls it a "103-unit student housing project" several times. A page later it calls it "a proposed 513-bed" community. City Weekly also reported 513 beds and four stories. That is about 5 beds per unit; the plan lists studio, one-bedroom and four-bedroom apartments.

2 · Date of the first vote

The Oct. 13 staff review says the survey area was adopted April 21, 2026. The city's transmittal, the draft resolution (Resolution 2026-03) and news reports say May 12, 2026.

3 · What's on the land

One section says the zone is R-1-10 and "existing uses … include single-family residential uses." Other sections say "vacant practice athletic fields" and "no existing residential population."

4 · Ogden City's total

The budget says Ogden City's 15-year total is $816,003. The public-benefit table says $818,003. That table is labeled "End-of-Area Collection," but its numbers are the 15-year totals.

5 · How long is the lease?

The plan and the Oct. 13 slides say a 99-year lease. The May 12 slides for the first vote say a 100-year lease (May 12 slides).

6 · Site costs

The plan lists site work at $4,616,778, with $250,000 for "unknowns." The Oct. 13 slide shows the same list with $500,000 for unknowns, for $4,866,778 (Oct. 13 slides).

7 · Tools that don't fit

The plan's list of possible tools includes home exterior loans, emergency home repair loans, down-payment help, and a program that turns apartments back into single-family homes. None seem to fit a new building on campus land.

The steps the law sets

Where this is in the process

  1. Spring 2023
    Weber State releases a request for proposals for student housing; Keller Development Group is picked (WSU letter, Oct. 28, 2024).
  2. Nov. 17, 2023
    Weber State's Board of Trustees revises the campus master plan for the project (same letter).
  3. Oct.–Nov. 2024
    Weber State asks the Utah Board of Higher Education to approve the partnership: about $46.8 million cost to the developer, no Weber State funding to the developer, completion summer 2027. The Commissioner recommends approval for the Nov. 21, 2024 meeting (Board of Higher Education).
  4. Dec. 23, 2024
    "Wildcat Square, LLC" is formed in Utah. Its manager is Seaver Management, LLC, the same manager as Keller Development Group, LLC (Utah business records).
  5. Oct. 2025
    The student housing project is presented to Weber State's Faculty Senate (The Signpost; Oct. 13 slides). The city's slides say Weber State ran the request for proposals and "Keller Development Group was awarded the RFP."
  6. Jan. 2026
    Keller Development Group and Weber State approach Ogden City staff about support (May 12 slides).
  7. Done · May 12, 2026
    RDA approves a survey area to study the site, 4–2 (City Weekly; Citizen Portal). We have not yet found posted minutes naming the two no votes.
  8. Done · Oct. 13, 2026
    Draft plan and budget go to the RDA board, and the board sets the hearing. One slide that night is titled "Development Activity is Underway on the Site" (photos not read) (Oct. 13 agenda; confirmed by Ogden City Council staff, Oct. 27).
  9. Done · Oct. 29, 2026
    Hearing notice posted. It says the plan and budget are open to the public at the Ogden City Recorder's Office (notice).
  10. Next · Nov. 17, 2026, 6 p.m.
    Public hearing. Anyone may speak or turn in written objections. The board then approves, changes or rejects the plan and budget. State law says the plan must be open to the public at least 30 days before the hearing; the draft resolution leaves that start date blank.
  11. Later
    Ogden School District, Weber County and Ogden City each vote on an interlocal agreement (a signed deal) for their share.
  12. Later
    City Council adopts the plan by ordinance; 30-day period to contest it. Plan says construction starts late 2026, tax money starts in 2028.
So far
The posted steps are moving in order, payments to the builder are written as after-the-fact ("post-performance"), and the budget has a hard cap. What the public can't see yet: the builder's money math, the lease terms, and the three interlocal agreements.

Who is connected, and why

Ogden City RDA board

The seven City Council members sit as the RDA board and vote on the plan and budget. The survey-area vote was 4–2 on May 12, 2026.

Hive page: Ogden and Weber County: Which Homes Are Short?

Weber State University

Owns the land (state land). Vice President Mark Halverson formally asked for the CRA in January 2026, per the plan.

Keller Development Group

Named in the plan as the builder, "selected through a competitive RFP," on a 99-year ground lease with the State of Utah. Its website calls it "a privately held investment company based in Utah"; CEO Casey Keller. It already owns Harrison Heights, 140 student beds in Ogden, and the Browning Apartments downtown (company website).

State records: Keller Development Group, LLC (formed Oct. 24, 2023) and Wildcat Square, LLC (formed Dec. 23, 2024) are both managed by Seaver Management, LLC, whose managers are Casey S. Keller and Marla R. Keller, of Bountiful (Utah Division of Corporations). Not on Utah's lobbyist register as of Oct. 27, 2026.

State campaign reports, 2016–2026: no gifts from Keller Development Group or Wildcat Square, LLC, and none from Casey or Marla Keller to any Ogden or Weber County official. Their personal gifts went to statewide and legislative races: Casey Keller, $1,000 to Attorney General Sean Reyes (July 2020); Marla Keller, $500 to Rep. Trevor Lee and $1,561.53 in three gifts to Phil Lyman's governor campaign (2024), and $250 to Protect Utah Values PAC (2024) (disclosures.utah.gov). Ogden council campaign reports are filed with Ogden City and were not part of this search.

Eric Gibson

Ogden School District
