One Firm, Every Hat: Zions' Grip on Utah's PID Market

Zions Bank isn't the biggest underwriter of Utah's Public Infrastructure District bonds — it's something more structural. Across deal after deal, it shows up as the feasibility-study author, the advisor to the government creating the district, and the trustee holding the money afterward. It says so itself, in writing.

Posted Aug. 30, 2026 · Page look updated Nov. 2, 2026

The Weber County Hive
01 — New to This?
Start Here

When a Utah city or county wants to create a Public Infrastructure District (PID), someone has to study whether it makes financial sense, advise the government on how to structure it, sell the resulting bonds to investors, and then hold the bond money afterward. Those are supposed to be separate jobs, done by separate parties, so no one entity is checking its own work.

On a striking number of Utah PIDs, one company keeps showing up in more than one of those roles: Zions Bank. And in at least one of its own client agreements, Zions has already put in writing that this overlap creates a financial incentive to steer business toward itself.

This isn't about Zions being the biggest underwriter — it isn't. It's about Zions being the firm most likely to be involved at nearly every stage of the same deal.

02 — What Zions Actually Does

Registered to be every party in the room

"Zions Bank Public Finance" (ZBPF) is a DBA of Zions Bancorporation, registered with the SEC and the MSRB as both a municipal advisor and a municipal securities dealer — meaning it's licensed to act as advisor, consultant, underwriter, or placement agent for the same kinds of government clients (though not underwriter and advisor on the identical bond issue at once, which federal rules prohibit). Its own materials say the firm has served as the State of Utah's own municipal advisor "for at least the past three decades," and that its capital markets desk "operates the only combined municipal underwriting and trading desk in Utah."

Across a Public Infrastructure District's life, Zions and its affiliates are positioned to fill seven separate roles — each one a distinct service line on Zions' own website:

Feasibility Study
Zions' own client agreements describe preparing studies on "capital facility plans... tax increment studies... public infrastructure districts" — the analysis that justifies creating a PID in the first place.
Municipal Advisor
Advises the city or county on how to structure the district and its financing.
Underwriter
Sells the resulting bonds to investors — on some, not all, Utah PID deals.
Direct Purchaser
"When there are no other buyers, Zions Bank steps forward by purchasing the bonds" itself, per its own description — becoming the investor of last resort.
Trustee
Zions' "Corporate Trust" affiliate holds and administers the bond proceeds after issuance.
Continuing Disclosure
Prepares and files the ongoing compliance reports that confirm a district is meeting its own disclosure obligations — including, in at least one case, reviewing and "restoring" a client's own past compliance.
Assessment Administration
Bills property owners, calculates payoffs, and — in Zions' own words — administers "lien releases" and "foreclosure" on the individual homeowners inside the district.

03 — In Zions' Own Words

The conflict, self-disclosed

This isn't an outside allegation. A real Zions Public Finance municipal advisory agreement — with the town of Springdale, Utah, dated May 4, 2023, and posted publicly on the town's own agenda site — states the structural conflict in its own boilerplate:

"Corporate Trust's desire to do business with CLIENT could create an incentive for Zions to recommend a course of action that increases the level of CLIENT's business activity with this affiliate." — Zions Public Finance municipal advisory agreement, Town of Springdale, May 4, 2023

In plain terms: Zions is telling its own government clients, in writing, that the advisor giving them recommendations has a financial reason to steer them toward hiring another arm of the same company. That's not a hidden pattern — it's disclosed language sitting inside a real contract, which raises the question of how often the incentive it describes actually plays out.

04 — How It Plays Out

The Nordic Village PID, step by step

One Weber County case shows the pattern in sequence, not just in theory.

MARCH 20, 2024
Keep the Valley PAC — a political committee connected to the Nordic Village development — pays Zions Bank $4,218.75, recorded as "Review of the feasibility study." The Nordic Village PID does not yet exist.
NOV. 20, 2024
Weber County creates the Nordic Village PID.
NOV. 28, 2025
Zions Bank is installed as trustee on the PID's $25 million bond issuance, replacing the original trustee, UMB Bank.

The firm that reviewed the project's feasibility before the district existed became the firm holding its bond money afterward. Nothing here shows a bidding process that considered other firms at either step — which is itself the point: a relationship built early carried forward without needing to compete for the later role.

It's not just Nordic Village

Black Desert PID (Ivins City, Washington County) — its own Governing Document names "Zions Public Finance, Inc." as financial consultant, even though a different firm, D.A. Davidson, underwrote the actual $106 million bond issue. Zions' advisory role recurs independent of who ends up underwriting.

Zions shows up on PID after PID

A check of five recent Utah PID bond indentures and governing documents shows Zions in a formal role on three of them, in different capacities each time:

Every one of the four PIDs checked here sold its bonds through the same firm, D.A. Davidson — the same underwriter already documented leading Utah's overall municipal bond rankings alongside Piper Sandler. On BZI Innovation Park specifically, D.A. Davidson's role is structured as placement agent rather than public underwriter, which typically means a more limited sale directly to qualified institutional buyers rather than a broad public offering — a real distinction worth keeping precise, not just a different word for the same job. Zions and D.A. Davidson show up together often enough across these four deals that neither firm's role can be fully understood without the other.

06 — The Role That Touches Homeowners Directly

Bills, payoffs, and foreclosure

Every role described so far is about structuring, selling, or holding the debt. One more role, "Special Assessment Administration," is about collecting it — directly from the people living inside the district, for as long as the assessment runs.

Zions describes the service itself: "assessment allocation, assessment reconciliation and review, billing, payoff calculations, assessment ordinance compliance and amendments, lien releases, foreclosure, and annual reports." This is the function that sends the bill every year, calculates what a homeowner owes if they want to pay off the balance early, and — if payments stop — administers the foreclosure process.

One example from Zions' own case studies shows how that obligation can outlast the person who originally agreed to it: at the Herriman Towne Center assessment area, a property transfer requires the outstanding assessment be paid in full unless the new owner consents to a non-judicial foreclosure remedy — in which case the debt can simply carry forward, owed by whoever owns the property next.

These three examples — Red Ledges (Wasatch County), Summit Mountain (Weber County), and Herriman Towne Center — are structured as special assessment areas, a related but not identical financing tool to a Public Infrastructure District. The billing-and-foreclosure administration role itself, however, is the same service line PIDs can also use.

07 — For Accuracy

The largest player. Not the only one.

Two other 2025 Utah PID indentures reviewed for this piece name different trustees entirely — worth stating plainly, because it's the more defensible and more interesting finding than a monopoly claim would be:

PIDTrustee
MIDA Cormont PIDU.S. Bank Trust Company
Point Phase 1 PID (Point of the Mountain)UMB Bank, N.A.

Point of the Mountain is a useful example of how that plays out in practice: Zions Public Finance led the consulting study that shaped the area's financing strategy — projecting revenue, evaluating potential tax-increment project areas, and assessing bonding capacity — before the Point Phase 1 PID's bonds were ever issued. A different firm, UMB Bank, ended up as trustee once those bonds actually closed. Zions didn't need to hold every seat at Point of the Mountain to have shaped how the deal got built.

Zions is not the only firm doing this kind of work in Utah, and it doesn't hold every seat on every deal. What the record supports is narrower and still significant: Zions holds the largest single financial-advisor market share in the state, by dollar volume, and it recurs across multiple PIDs in overlapping roles more often than any other firm checked here — a pattern its own contract language acknowledges creates a financial incentive, whether or not that incentive is ever acted on in a given case.
