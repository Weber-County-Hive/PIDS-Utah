1781 Gibson Ave.: Ogden's RDA, Lync and the $7.4 million appraisal

Ogden's Redevelopment Agency (RDA) plans to sell about 10.8 acres it assembled at and near 1781 Gibson Ave. to Lync Development, LLC for $5.1 million. The RDA's own appraisal valued the land at $7.4 million. In 2023, the RDA paid $725,000 for one house on the site, appraised at $446,762.

Agency: Ogden City Redevelopment AgencyBoard: Ogden City CouncilWork session: Tue., Nov. 10, 2026, 4 p.m.

Posted Nov. 5, 2026 · Updated Nov. 10, 2026

WHERE
Gibson Grove area, Ogden: about 10.8 acres at and near 1781 Gibson Ave., along the Ogden River
  • Seller: Ogden City Redevelopment Agency (the City Council sits as its board)
  • Proposed buyer: Lync Development, LLC; the draft agreement names Pat Burns its "sole and exclusive representative"
  • When: City Council and RDA joint work session, Tue., Nov. 10, 2026, 4 p.m., Council Work Room, 2549 Washington Blvd., and Zoom. The regular RDA meeting that night is cancelled; the work session is still on.

In plain words

Ogden's redevelopment agency has been buying houses and lots near Gibson Avenue since at least 2014 (city records give 2013 and 2015) to put together one big piece of land for new homes. Now it wants to sell all of it to one builder. The agency hired an appraiser, who said the land is worth $7.4 million if it can be built on the way the city's request allowed. The builder that won is offering $5.1 million and would pay most of that over four years on a loan from the agency. Back in 2023, the agency paid $278,238 more than the appraisal for one duplex on the site, saying the whole area could later sell for more than it cost.

Explain it like I'm 10

  • Ogden's RDA is a city agency. It has been buying houses and lots on and near Gibson Avenue.
  • Now it wants to sell all of that land, about 10.8 acres, to one builder, Lync Development.

  • In 2023 the RDA paid $725,000 for one duplex there. The appraisal on that duplex was $446,762.
  • The council talks about the sale at a work session on Tuesday, Nov. 10, at 4 p.m.

The short version

  • The Nov. 10 staff report proposes selling about 10.8 acres (8 parcels) to Lync Development, LLC for $5.1 million: $500,000 at settlement and $4.6 million on a 3% note the RDA carries, paid over 4 years.
  • The RDA's own March 2026 appraisal: $7.4 million under zoning up to R-3. Staff wrote that "the offer price provided by all bids, including the winning bid, was below the R-3 appraised value."
  • In 2023 the RDA voted 6–0 to pay $725,000 for a duplex at 1781 Gibson appraised at $446,762, from its Housing Fund.
  • The city and RDA have spent more than $1.8 million buying and clearing the area.
  • Lync companies already appear in other Weber County records the Hive has read, including $12.8 million in public payments, most of it from Plain City's Sage Creek financing district as reimbursements under its approved agreements.

What is the RDA Housing Fund?

Utah law makes redevelopment agencies set aside part of the tax increment they receive as a "housing allocation." Owners in a project area pay the full tax rate and the county treasurer collects it; the agreed share of the growth in value, for the agreed term, is then allocated to the agency's project area instead of the other taxing entities' budgets. The housing share goes into a fund like Ogden's RDA Housing Fund.

Utah Code 17C-1-412 lists what a housing allocation may be used for. Most uses center on "income targeted housing": paying for land or construction of it, rehabilitating it, lending to others for it, or planning it. Other listed uses include costs "related to housing" in a project area where a board has found a "development impediment," and replacing housing units lost to project area development.

The 2023 purchase used Housing Fund money. The 2026 plan is about 140 homes sold at market rate. The records the Hive has read do not say which listed use the 2023 purchase was made under. That's a question for Tuesday, not a finding.

The commissioner-elect, Lync and the lobbyists

  • Jon Beesley was Plain City mayor from January 2018 to January 2026. On Nov. 3, 2026 he was elected to the Weber County Commission (he ran unopposed) and takes office in January. Separately, before Beesley was on the commission, the 2024 Weber County Commission signed Burns's Longhorn Estates agreement and appointed Burns to the LMSA board.
  • His 2025 Plain City disclosure form, filed as mayor, lists $5,000+ income from Lync Construction, "consulted on projects." It also lists his own JDB Company LLC, "consulting on construction projects." His Weber County candidate disclosure form (January 2026) lists no employer and no Lync.
  • A tip came in: Beesley's reply to a voter who asked about his work for Lync, checked with the voter, says, "I worked there for 1 year from 2024-2025. I was a superintendent of the Hooper irrigation water meter installation project..."
  • Oct. 16, 2025: with Beesley presiding, the Planning Commission reported to the Plain City Council on Lync's Sage Creek subdivision (30 lots). The minutes show no disclosure and no recusal. For context: his 2025 Plain City disclosure form, filed as mayor, does list Lync income, and his own statement says the Lync job ended in 2025.
  • Plain City hired Lincoln Hill Partners on Dec. 1, 2021; its partners include Kerry Gibson, Casey Hill and Lincoln Shurtz. Gibson's own lobbyist registration (NRAE Solutions) lists Plain City as a client from Sept. 7, 2023. On May 15, 2025, with Beesley presiding, the council approved $42,000 for 2025 to Gibson's Legislative Solutions. On Jan. 15, 2026, after Beesley left office, the council voted 3–2 to end that contract.

The same lobbyist works for Ogden

  • Kerry Gibson's registration lists Ogden City as a client since Jan. 19, 2024. Ogden pays RRJ Consulting, and RRJ's invoices list Gibson's work at $30,000 (February 2024) and $40,000 (April 2025).
  • Ogden City paid Xalisco, LLC (Kerry Gibson is a member, with Luis Octavio Lopez and David Lopez-Carrillo) about 37 times, about $14,000, from July 2023 to May 2026, including $9,800 for the Mayor's State of the City event. Mayor Ben Nadolski's 2023 campaign paid Xalisco $3,187.50.
  • Ogden told the Hive it made no payments to Kerry Gibson directly. The city pays RRJ Consulting, and RRJ pays him, so city records don't show what his work covers or whether it touches this deal. The Ogden City Council also sits as the RDA board.

Stephanie Russell, Pat Burns and the LMSA sewer district

  • Little Mountain Service Area (LMSA) is the sewer and water district for the West Weber corridor, where Lync and Burns hold land.
  • Stephanie Russell is Weber County's Economic Development Director, LMSA's manager, and LMSA's registered lobbyist through her own Sage Development Group (registered Sept. 18, 2024; LMSA is her one client).
  • June 25, 2024: at the same county meeting, the LMSA ARPA sewer agreement she presented was approved and Pat Burns was appointed to LMSA's board.
  • July 15, 2024: Burns told LMSA his company would fund sewer lines "with the assumption that LMSA or Central Weber Sewer will take it over."
  • March 11, 2025: LMSA resolutions annexed the "Longhorn I" and "Longhorn II" parcels owned by 2020, LLC into the district (Burns signed the 2024 Longhorn Estates agreement with the county for 2020, LLC). The petitioner and owner were the same, so no hearing was required.

Questions to ask, and whom to ask

  • Utah Legislature / your state legislators: Under Utah's infrastructure financing district law, who may sit on a district's board before residents live there, and should a board member be able to vote on reimbursements to his own company?
  • Ogden City Council / RDA board (Nov. 10 work session): Why sell for $5.1 million when the RDA's own appraisal is $7.4 million? What did the other three offers propose, and how were they scored?
  • RDA board: Why is the RDA financing $4.6 million of the price at 3% instead of the buyer using a bank?
  • RDA board: In 2023 the RDA paid $278,238 over appraisal, saying the assembled land could later sell for more than it cost. Does this sale do that?
  • Ogden Community & Economic Development: Which Housing Fund use did the 2023 purchase fall under, and will any of the 140 homes be income-targeted?
  • RDA board: How will the RDA make sure the homes go to owners who live in them, and who builds the bridge?
  • Ogden Community & Economic Development (Eric Gibson, staff contact): Who made the unsolicited 2025 offer that came with the $3 million appraisal?
  • Ogden City Council / RDA board (Nov. 10 work session or public comment): When, from whom and for how much did the RDA buy each of the other six parcels? Will the itemized expense list be posted with the agenda?
  • Ogden City Council / RDA board (Nov. 10 work session or public comment): Who owns Lync Development, LLC, the proposed buyer?
